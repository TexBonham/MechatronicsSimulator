## 1. Pulled-out stepper runaway (SIG-033)

X2 observed: a stepper that pulled out while its shaft was free (held by a brake, or the shaft spinning after a
power cut and re-enable) diverges once re-enabled. Derive the mechanism from the code before changing it: after
pull-out the commanded angle re-synchronizes to the shaft angle rounded to a step, but with the predicted lead
delta_p = theta_cmd - (theta + omega h) a fast-spinning shaft (|omega h| > 2 step angles) re-synchronizes every
tick while the drive rest is set a full lead ahead of the shaft, so the drive keeps pumping energy in the direction
of motion instead of braking it. Required behaviour (write it into the SIG-033 row): re-synchronization sets the
commanded angle to the step nearest the *predicted* shaft angle and the published drive must never add energy:
while |omega h| exceeds one step angle the stepper is in loss of synchronism, publishes the drive with rest at the
predicted angle minus sign(omega) x one step (a restoring torque against the motion, capped by the torque-speed
table at |omega|), counts missed steps, and only when |omega h| falls below one step angle does normal tracking
resume. A disabled stepper publishes no drive (unchanged). Reference tests, derived in the comments: (a) a free
shaft spinning at 50 steps per tick with the motor enabled and zero commanded speed decelerates monotonically to
rest within the torque-speed table's envelope (energy strictly decreasing each tick in the emulated implicit
spring, as the A1 tests emulate it), never diverging; (b) a shaft released from a brake hold with the command at
the held position stays within one step; (c) re-enable after a power cut mid-move (command frozen, shaft moved on)
re-synchronizes and settles, missed steps counted equal to the rounded displacement.

## 2. Drive torque apportioning (ruling 23, KER-027)

The layer combines drives on one joint as parallel springs; the physics returns one torque for the combined drive.
Apportion the read-back to each component by its own spring law at the published state: tau_i = K_i (r_i - theta)
- D_i omega, using the same theta and omega the components used, scaled so that the sum equals the read-back torque
(the scale corrects the implicit-solve difference equally). The stepper's pull-out and the brake's slip each see
their own torque. Test with a stepper and a brake on one joint: sum of apportioned torques equals the combined
torque exactly; with the brake released the stepper's share equals the whole. Row text for the combination rule in
sig.md (X2 could not edit it) citing KER-027 and ruling 23.

## 3. Load cell on a fixed joint (SIG-014, ruling 24)

Ruling 24 builds a payload attached at t = 0 as a fixed child body of the hook, so the load cell's `senses.payload`
must read the carrier joint's reaction force when the payload is a fixed child: add `senses` mode `joint_reaction`
(index of the fixed joint; reads `state.joints[index].reaction` force magnitude, the parent-on-child reaction the
bridge publishes) beside `payload` (weld force) and `joint` (generalized force). Test with a synthetic state: a
2 kg child under gravity reads 19.62 N. Binding line if `make_signal_component` needs the new key.
