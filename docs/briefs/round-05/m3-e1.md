## 1. Collision detection (ENG-016, 017, 018, 019)

New `collision.hpp/.cpp`: narrow phase for every pair among sphere, capsule, box, cylinder (as capsule-with-flat-
ends or box approximation: state which and record it), plane, and convex hull (GJK plus EPA for penetration depth
and normal, in-house: no library in the runtime, ENG-018); a broad phase by axis-aligned bounding boxes. Contact
record: bodies, point, normal (from a to b), depth. Any other geometry kind ends the run with an error naming it
(ENG-017). Reference oracle (ENG-019): closed-form contact sets for sphere-sphere, sphere-plane, box-plane (each
corner), capsule-plane, capsule-capsule, box-box face and edge cases, hull-plane, with depth and normal derived in
the test; propose the ENG-019 tolerance (1e-9 m depth, 1e-6 rad normal) in the spec row. The Python reference
library (hppfcl or fcl) may be added under the dev group and used only from `tests/` if it installs through uv on
Linux; if it does not, the closed-form set is the oracle and the row says so.

## 2. Soft contact, implicit, with friction (ENG-020, 021; R-06)

Contact force per contact: normal stiffness K_n and damping C_n from the model's `ContactModel` (per pair or
default), force = K_n depth + C_n depth_rate along the normal, never adhesive (clamped at zero); Coulomb friction
with the model's mu, regularized (viscous below a slip-velocity threshold stated in the config), integrated
implicitly: contribute K and D blocks to `DynamicsTerms` (the step's `(H + hD + h^2 K)`) so stiff contacts do not
limit the step. Contacts enter the provider's `evaluate` through the new files; outputs fill
`PhysicsOutputs.contacts` with force on body a.

References (derive each in the test; propose the ENG-021 tolerances in the row): a 1 kg sphere dropped from
0.1 m onto a plane with K_n = 1e5 N/m, C_n = 1e3 N s/m comes to rest with steady penetration m g / K_n = 9.81e-5 m
within 1 percent and no residual bounce above 1e-6 m after 1 s; a body driven into a hard stop at constant force F
stops with penetration F / K_n; a box resting on a 10 degree incline with mu = 0.5 stays (tan 10 degrees < mu) and
slides at mu = 0.1 with acceleration g (sin 10 degrees - mu cos 10 degrees) within 1 percent; the sphere drop is
bitwise identical on a second run (ENG-023).

## 3. Payload change in the engine (ENG-035)

A payload is a body attached to a carrier by a fixed constraint (weld) that can be disabled (release) and
re-enabled at the carrier's pose; mass change rescales the body's inertia; both as mid-run calls on the provider
(`set_body_mass`, `set_attachment`), applied before the next step. Reference: a 2 kg payload welded to a held
carrier shows constraint force m g; released, it falls g t^2 / 2 = 0.1962 m in 0.2 s within 1 percent.
