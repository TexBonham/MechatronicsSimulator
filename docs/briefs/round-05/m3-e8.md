# M3-E8: a rope segment going taut inside a step (the start-up blow's cause, ENG-047)

Why: brief B3 traced the kilonewton blow in a rope-hung payload's first milliseconds to the engine's rope: each
segment is decided slack or taut at the start of the step and held for the step, and a segment exactly at its
rest length counts as slack. A rope at its rest length therefore holds nothing in the first step, tightens one
segment per step, and the load falls for N steps onto it: 3750 N for a 1 kg load on an eight-segment rope of
E A = 500 kN, where the physics gives at most 2 m g = 20 N for a release at rest length, and
m g (1 + sqrt(1 + 2 d k / (m g))) for a drop d onto a slack rope of stiffness k. B3 made a hanging payload
start at its static stretch, which avoids the rule for a load at rest; any rope that goes slack and taut during
a run (the gantry's G16 start, where the hook dips as the lift belt takes up its load; any swing that unloads
the rope) still gets the blow. A unilateral spring's state must be consistent with the state the step ends in.
