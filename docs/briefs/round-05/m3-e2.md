## 1. Member end-loads (ENG-030, PHY-007)

For every lumped member, every step: the three forces and three moments at each end, in the member's root frame,
from the segment springs and the constraint reactions at the root and end supports; published in `outputs()` per
member in neutral order (shape agreed with the kernel's `PhysicsOutputs.members`: start and end wrenches).
Reference: a cantilever with a static tip load P: root moment P L and root shear P within 1e-9 relative after
settling (damped); a simply supported beam under midspan P: end reactions P / 2 each, zero end moments.

## 2. Mid-run changes (ENG-031 to ENG-034, PHY-008)

Provider calls applied before the next step: `set_spring_offset(member, segment, offset)` (permanent, a yield),
`set_spring_stiffness(member, segment, k)` (buckling collapse: milestone 4 uses it; implement the call),
`disable_constraint(index)` (fastener break: milestone 4 uses it), `release_load(attachment)` (rope snap or payload
drop: shares E1's attachment mechanism if it lands first; otherwise a fixed-constraint disable). Each change is
recorded in the step health or an event list the adapter can read. References: an offset on a cantilever's root
spring shifts the static tip deflection by offset times L within 1e-9 relative; halving a segment stiffness
changes the static deflection by the closed-form amount for that segment's contribution (derive from the
segment chain).

## 3. Euler buckling (ENG-042)

A pinned-pinned lumped column (N segments, planar) under axial compression loses stability at the Euler load
pi^2 E I / L^2: with N = 8 the lumped critical load is within 2 percent of Euler's (derive the discrete value
from the chain's stiffness matrix eigenvalue: state it) and the test shows stability below and divergence above
by the sign of the smallest eigenvalue of the tangent stiffness, not by running to divergence.

## 4. Rope as a lumped chain (ENG-047, PHY-004; ADR 0016 proposal, ruling 2)

`Rope{from_body, from_point, to_body, to_point, segments, EA, damping, mass_per_length, rest_length,
breaking_tension}`: N point masses (free translational bodies or a dedicated chain state, say which and why),
N + 1 unilateral springs with k = EA / l_segment and viscous damping, force only in tension (slack below the
segment rest length), implicit in the step like the beam springs (ADR 0013), rest_length changeable per step
(winch); tension per segment published every step (PHY-004); snap when tension exceeds breaking_tension for one
step: the rope's end attachment is released (ENG-034) and an event recorded. References: a rope pendulum of
length L with a point mass M at the end and negligible rope mass has period 2 pi sqrt(L / g) within 0.5 percent at
small amplitude with N = 4 (derive the added-mass correction for mass_per_length > 0 and test it at 1 percent);
a hanging rope under its own weight has tension at the top equal to its weight within 1e-9 relative; a slack rope
(end closer than rest length) carries zero tension; a step load above breaking_tension releases the load within one
step.

## 5. Belt compliance (ENG-043 stiffness, ruling 10)

Verify the transmission's stiffness path makes a belt a spring between shaft and carriage (not rigid): a carriage
of mass m on a belt of stiffness k with the shaft held has natural frequency sqrt(k / m) / (2 pi) within 1
percent; the stiffness must be implicit (no omega h limit at 1 kHz for the gantry's placeholder stiffness; state
the envelope). If the path is already correct, the reference test and the contract line are the deliverable.
