# M3-E7: a moving load on a flexible member (load-path coupling, ruling 63)

Why: the gantry cases found that a carriage joint on a flexible rail loads the rail's parent body, not the beam.
The lumped member hangs off its parent as a chain of node bodies; the carriage is another child of the same
parent. The rails therefore carry only their own weight, and G15's stress, safety factor and deflection never see
the payload. Ruling 63 (planned default A): load-path coupling in the engine. Full coupling (the carriage riding
the deflected beam through a sliding constraint between node bodies) is recorded for milestone 4.

## The model

Data: a flexible member names the joints it carries (design: a list on the member's `flexible` block; neutral
model: the carried child bodies on `LumpedMember`; engine: the same on the lumped beam). Validation at build: a
carried joint's parent body is the member's parent; the joint's origin, over its whole travel (its limits),
projects onto the member's span [0, L] (a build error otherwise, naming the joint and the excursion); a joint is
carried by at most one member.

Each step, for each carried joint with child body C: the engine has the joint's reaction wrench, parent on child,
[moment about the child origin; force], world-aligned (`JointOutput::reaction_wrench`). The carriage's load on
the rail is its negative: force F = -f, moment M = -m about the child origin r_c. Project r_c onto the member
axis (root frame x, ruling 24): s = (r_c - r_root) . x, p the foot point, d = r_c - p. Between the adjacent nodes
k and k + 1 at s_k <= s <= s_{k+1}, segment length l: weights w_k = (s_{k+1} - s) / l, w_{k+1} = (s - s_k) / l
(the lever rule: statically equivalent to F at p for the transverse components). The moment about p is
M_p = M + d x F; put its bending components on the same two nodes with the same weights. Apply to the parent body
the exact opposite of everything applied to the nodes, at the same points, so the pair is internal: zero net force
and moment on the system, and the parent now receives the load through the beam's ends. Reroute only the
components the lumped chain carries (read which bending planes and which end conditions it has, rulings 16, 25,
33; the axial force and the torsion stay on the parent directly if the chain has no such degree of freedom):
state exactly which in the contract and the ENG row. The carriage's own motion is unchanged: it does not ride the
deflected rail (the approximation; publish per member the largest deflection at a carried joint over the run as
the position error this ignores, through the translation's approximation lines and an engine output).

Timing: the reaction used is the one the engine has at the start of the step (the previous step's, or computed
from the start-of-step state: say which); at load, a carriage at rest must put its static weight on the beam from
the first step. Stability, derive in the header: along a direction in which the parent can move, the parent's
solve still holds the carriage's inertia implicitly and the rerouted pair returns it one step late, a fixed-point
iteration with factor m_c / (m_p + m_c) < 1, so it converges for any mass ratio; state the lag error for a
constant acceleration.

## Tests (derive each expected value in the test header; these are oracles, not regression values)

1. Statics, a carriage of weight P at rest at position a on a span L, b = L - a, after the beam's settling time
   (derive it from its node damping): pinned-pinned deflection under the load P a^2 b^2 / (3 E I L) at a = L / 2,
   L / 4 and 0.1 L within the 2 percent row at eight segments; member end shear reactions P b / L and P a / L
   within 1e-9 relative (the lever rule and statics are exact); clamped-clamped at mid-span P L^3 / (192 E I) and
   end moments P L / 8 within the row at sixteen segments; cantilever with the carriage at the tip
   P L^3 / (3 E I).
2. The parent is unchanged: the total reaction from the world on the parent equals the sum of the weights within
   1e-9, with and without the member carrying the joint; with the carriage exactly at a node the whole load is on
   that node; at an end node the beam bends by nothing.
3. A carriage moving slowly at constant speed across the span: the recorded deflection under the load follows
   P a^2 b^2 / (3 E I L) within the row plus the dynamic term you derive for that speed; crossing a node boundary
   leaves no jump in the node forces larger than the per-step change of the weights.
4. A carriage accelerating along the member while the parent accelerates across it: the parent's motion with
   and without the rerouting agree within the lag bound you derived; no energy gain with everything at rest (the
   pair does no net work on a rigid configuration).
5. Validation errors: wrong parent, travel leaving the span, a joint carried twice.
6. Through the native adapter from a small data-only design: the member end loads, the node-pose deflection (the
   integrity check's reading, do not edit that package) and a run-twice identical archive (invariant 4).
7. The gantry's cases must not change while no member carries a joint: one existing native case bit-identical
   before and after.
